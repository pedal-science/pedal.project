The pedal's noise: codec and Core only

11 October 2026 · board 1 and every input and output circuit disconnected · LIN1 jumpered to the codec's own GND · Pico removed

These are the measurements that need nothing plugged in: the WM8960 codec on its jumpers to the Core host, its left input shorted at the pin so the converter sees silence, and namchaind on the Core. Nothing else is attached — no board 1, no jacks, no output fixture, no interface. Every capture is of the codec's own silence, so all of the noise below is the pedal's.

How to read it. Each capture shows a spectrum in dBFS per 0.73 Hz bin, and the same noise as octave-band bars. Hum is a spike at 50 Hz and its harmonics; hiss is a broad slope across the high bands. The captures were deleted on 11 October, once this report had been read, so the page carries no audio: the graphs and the numbers below are the record, and the two players that stood here were the only part of it that needed the files.

The quietest state measured here is the engine at 1024-frame periods, at -79.9 dBFS(A); the loudest is the mains-hum reference at -65.3 dBFS(A).

What the graphs show

  • No mains hum in any of the codec-only captures. There is no 50 Hz line to find in the first four spectra — the largest line below 1 kHz in the engine's own capture is the 750 Hz period-rate artefact, not hum. The hum appears only in the reference capture, which is the one with board 1 attached.
  • The engine's capture path carries a period-rate artefact. At 64-frame periods there is a line at 750.0 Hz with harmonics at 1500, 2250 and 3000 Hz, each with ±0.7 Hz sidebands, and the 1–4 kHz bands sit at the same level as that line. At 1024-frame periods the whole family moves to 46.875 Hz and its harmonics and falls 13–15 dB; the floor improves 4.3 dB A-weighted. A plain arecord at the same 64-frame periods shows the same 750 Hz line 18 dB lower, so the boundary is real and the engine's handling of it is what is worse.
  • The high-frequency hiss depends on the playback state. With no playback stream open, 4–16 kHz holds over 90% of the energy; open a silent playback stream and it drops 15 dB. That is a property of this converter's capture-only state, not of the pedal's input.
  • Magnitude. The quietest configuration in these graphs is -79.7 dBFS rms (-79.9 dB(A)); for scale, 2a's record for this converter with its input shorted is −90.6 dBFS unweighted and −93.5 dB(A), and this host's own record from 2026-10-06 is −88.0 dB(A).

Raw captures, the session log and every earlier test: troubleshooting-log.md and README.md. Numbers on this page are in noise-numbers.json; the page and graphs are built by make-document.py. Conventions: dBFS at 1.0 V rms full scale, 20 Hz–20 kHz, channel 0 = LIN1, and a 2-channel capture throughout (an 8-channel capture of the same signal reads 11–12 dB higher because of the interface itself).

Sample format. The card offers S16_LE S24_LE S32_LE with SAMPLE_BITS: [16 32] and rates of 8–48 kHz, and no float format: I2S and the WM8960's DAI are integer PCM. These captures are 32-bit containers holding 24-bit data — the bottom eight bits are zero in every sample of every capture here, which is the converter's own resolution, left-justified. namchaind asks for S24_3LE, S32_LE, S16_LE in that order and takes the first the device offers, which on this card is S32_LE (4 bytes per sample). 16-bit would quantise at about −96 dBFS, right where this session is measuring, so it would mask the noise rather than show it.
