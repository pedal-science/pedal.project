# E2: the four measurements, and what they leave the tiers

**Date:** 2026-10-06
**Question** (plan.md 3.8, `docs/rig-switch-strategies.md`): the tier design came out of E1, which
measured **one model plus a cab IR**. Four things it did not measure are E2: Lite against Full,
catch-up for chains, warm density without IRs, and an IR kept as a record of its input.
**In one line:** the switching machinery holds up better than the plan assumed — catch-up needs
**170 ms per model** rather than 340, the work is the `d(d+1)/2` replays it predicted, **8 Full or 30
Lite** models stay warm on one core, and an IR can be kept as a record and filled **exactly** — but
**tier 2's premise does not hold**: Lite and Full are two independently trained models 9 to 40 dB
apart, so "a Lite takes over at once" plays a *different-sounding* rig until the Full catches up.

**Still open, and it needs your ears:** whether that difference, for the tens of ms a catch-up takes,
is audible. `build/e2/audition/` has the files; the protocol is at the end of this note.

Evidence (namchain `083de32`, the bench's four new modes):

- [`bench-results/e2-lite-vs-full.txt`](../../namchain/bench-results/e2-lite-vs-full.txt) — 52 models,
  1144 judged handovers.
- [`bench-results/e2-chains-and-irs.txt`](../../namchain/bench-results/e2-chains-and-irs.txt) — depths
  1–4 against replay length, and the IR fill.
- [`bench-results/e2-warm-density-vip.txt`](../../namchain/bench-results/e2-warm-density-vip.txt) —
  what stays warm on one core, on vip.

Input throughout: [guitar-di.wav](../guitar-di.wav), 48 kHz mono 24-bit, 56.66 s, peak −1.32 dBFS,
rms −23.64 dBFS, md5 `f9ab7f8f7ece122917d9701c3360a32f`. It was recorded for this and its level was
checked not to matter: trimming it 22.7 dB moved the relative null by 1.4 dB.

## 1. Lite against Full: the premise is false, the crossfade is not

Every model in the library, rendered twice (its 3-channel submodel and its 8-channel one) and
compared sample by sample:

| | worst | median | best |
|---|---|---|---|
| null, dB below the signal | **−9.2 dB** | −21.7 dB | −40.1 dB |
| null, absolute | −26.6 dBFS | −44.1 dBFS | −60.3 dBFS |

They are **level-matched** (median 0.09 dB apart) and **time-aligned** (the best integer lag is 0 for
50 of 52 models, 1 sample for the rest), so what is left is timbre. The worst is the `Ampeg SVT VR /
8x10 / Beta52A + DI` capture at 9 dB below the signal — a plainly different sound — and the best are
the nine SD-1 pedal captures at 35–40 dB down. It is worst exactly where it matters: amps, amp+cabs
and full-rigs all sit at a median of −21 dB.

The reason is in the files: a `SlimmableContainer` here holds **two separately trained networks**,
not one network at two widths. In the worst model 56 of the Lite's 1871 weights appear in the Full's
(3%); in the vendored reference `A2.nam`, 4 of 1871 (0.2%). namchain's own daemon doc already says
so about the budget path — `model_slimmed` is named because "a Lite does not sound like the Full the
rig names".

**The crossfade itself is fine.** 102 of 104 model-directions passed every glitch rule at all 11
switch points; the two exceptions are continuity findings 20–40% over the second-difference bar at
55 s, where the DI is a decaying tail (oracle peak −36 dBFS). Where the detector flagged something
at a musical level — an HF excess of 3.4–3.9 dB on two SVT models — the excess is at −95 dBFS
absolute, 73 dB below the signal. So handing over does not click; it *changes the sound*.

What that costs tier 2: "a Lite copy always warm takes over at once, Full catches up in the
background, then a 5 ms crossfade to Full" means the player hears the Lite — 9 to 40 dB away from
the rig they selected — for the whole catch-up. The plan's own contingency says it: *"If it can be
heard, tier 2 becomes tier 1 or catch-up."*

## 2. Catch-up for chains: 170 ms per model, not 340

Four different models in series, each stage reset and caught up by the procedure the tiers imply
(stage *k* gets the tail of the true input, or of the stage before it, so stage *k* costs
`(d−k+1) × replay` samples and the chain costs `d(d+1)/2` replays):

| replay per model | depth 1 | depth 2 | depth 3 | depth 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 85 ms | −115.3 | −144.5 | **−50.1** | **−57.8** |
| 170 ms | exact | exact | exact | exact |
| 340 ms | exact | exact | exact | exact |
| 680 ms | exact | exact | exact | exact |

dBFS error in the 200 ms after the switch; "exact" is bit-identical to the chain that ran all along.
**170 ms per model is exact at every depth** — the per-model replay does not have to grow with depth,
which is what "d × 340 ms" assumed. 85 ms is enough at depth 1–2 and fails at 3–4, right at the
−60 dBFS bar.

The work is confirmed: the measured cost is exactly `d(d+1)/2` single-model replays, on vip
**9.8 ms** at depth 1, **29.4 ms** at depth 2, **58.8 ms** at depth 3, **98.2 ms** at depth 4 (170 ms
per model, low priority, no isolation). Tier 2's "Full within tens of ms" is true to depth 2 and
optimistic at 3–4.

E1's 340 ms is also explained: a series chain's memory is the **sum** of its stages', not the longest
— 6347 samples for the model plus 8192 for the cab is 303 ms — which is why its 8192-sample default
replay left −51 dBFS. The bench's default replay was `max()` of the two and is now explicit.

## 3. Warm density: 8 Full or 30 Lite per core

On vip, live rig on core 3 (model + cab), candidates on core 2 without cabs, q64 (1333 µs):

| candidates | warm p99, % of a quantum | blocks behind | | candidates | warm p99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Full | 6.6 | 1 | | 10 Lite | 18.1 |
| 2 Full | 13.9 | 1 | | 20 Lite | 58.5 |
| 4 Full | 30.4 | 1 | | **30 Lite** | **97.4** |
| 6 Full | 56.7 | 1 | | 40 Lite | 141.2 |
| **8 Full** | **89.1** | 1 | | 50 Lite | 181.4 |
| 10 Full | 112.9 | 6 | | | |
| 12 Full | 139.7 | 2621 | | | |

The bar is E1's: the warm thread's p99 under one quantum, meaning it is tracking the live input
rather than falling behind. **8 Full** or **30 Lite** clear it; 10 Full and 40 Lite do not, and at
12 Full the thread is 3.5 s behind and never recovers. The plan's "~10 Full or ~50 Lite per core" is
25% and 40% optimistic, and the reason is the same cache crowding the two-amp result found: the
first Full costs 6.6% of a quantum and the eighth costs 11.1%.

The audio pays little: with 8 Full candidates the render core's p99 rises 8.08% → 10.60% and its max
11.7% → 24.2%, with no overruns in any run. A spinner saturating the warm core at a lower priority
changes nothing measurable (89.1% → 94.3%), so this is also the "housekeeping core under load" case.

## 4. An IR as a record of its input: exact

Run the model over the whole input to make the record, run the cab over that record once as the
reference, then at each of 9 switch points reset the cab and fill it from the last 8192 samples:
**−138.5 dBFS worst** (float rounding in the partitioned FFT; some points bit-identical), and the
fill costs **1.26 ms** on vip — 2.5% of the 50 ms a switch has to fit in. The optimisation is sound
and cheap. It only applies to rigs with a separate IR (35 of the 52 library models carry their own
cab), and it is an optimisation, not a requirement.

## What the tiers can now be

**Proposal, for the decision this note is waiting on.** The numbers say tier 1 and tier 2 are both
affordable, but not for the same things:

- **Tier 1 (Full always warm, ~11 ms, exact)** at **8 models per warm core**. One warm core (core 2)
  therefore holds 8 Fulls: the one-press candidates — the footswitch neighbours and the assignable
  target — with room to spare at two or three rigs of up to three models.
- **Tier 2 (Lite warm, Full catches up)** at **30 models per core**, and the catch-up costs 29 ms at
  depth 2 and 59 ms at depth 3, which is affordable on a housekeeping core. A bank of 8 rigs × 3
  models is 24 instances: it fits tier 2 and does not fit tier 1.
- **Tier 3 (loaded, not running)** is unchanged, and with 170 ms per model its Lite-first catch-up is
  ~1/3 of the Full cost above.
- **The 16 × 16 case fits nothing**, as the doc already says: 240 instances at 30 Lite per core is
  five cores.

So the shape the measurements support is what the doc already sketches — tier 1 for the one-press
candidates, tier 2 for the rest of the bank — **provided** the Lite standing in for the Full during
a catch-up is acceptable. If it is not, the bank has to be tier 1, and 8 models per core is the
ceiling: three rigs of three, not eight.

## The decision, and what it needs

One question, and it is a listening one: **play `build/e2/audition/<model>-full.wav` against
`<model>-lite.wav`, then `<model>-handover.wav`.** If the Lite is audibly a different rig, tier 2's
first step is an audible fault and the tiers go to tier 1 (8 models per core) with catch-up for the
rest. If it is not — the difference in the files is 9 to 40 dB down, and the loudest of them is a
heavily distorted SVT capture — tier 2 stands as designed and the density numbers above are the
limits the service enforces.

Files are per model: `-full`, `-lite`, and `-handover` (the two alternating with a 5 ms crossfade at
each switch). The set is the worst, the median, the best, E1's model, and the two the detector
flagged. Read `build/e2/audition/README.md` first.

## What this does not settle

- The bank's real composition — how many rigs, how many instances each — is the rigs you build, not
  this table.
- The mixed case: a warm set of 8 Full *and* some Lite on the same core is what the tiers imply and
  has not been run.
- Whether the engine's `SetSlimmableSize` path has the same buffer-sizing quirk the bench's
  `--slim` had: a Lite candidate came out using *more* resident memory than a Full in this bench.
  Not decision-relevant (either way it is megabytes against vip's 1 GB), but it is a loose thread.
