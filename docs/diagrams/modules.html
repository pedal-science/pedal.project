The ground rules this map is checked against

  1. One tree. Every board has exactly one cable that carries ground, towards the root. Several ground wires in that one cable are good (the clocks need them); a second route between two boards is a loop.
  2. The audio star is on the codec carrier: its analogue ground bus, on the codec's J5 GND pin. Each audio module reaches it in the cable that carries its signal.
  3. The Pi's side and the audio side meet once: on the codec board's own ground plane, between its digital pins (where the I2S grounds land) and its analogue pins (where the bus is).
  4. A jack's sleeve travels with its tip, twisted or screened, to its module — never on its own wire to the star as well.
  5. No ground in the relay coil cable, the MIDI DIN wires, the battery-sense wires or the toggle's wires.
  6. The case touches nothing: plastic jacks, nylon standoffs, insulated USB-C and display mounts — except, if you fit it, one jumper at the audio star.
